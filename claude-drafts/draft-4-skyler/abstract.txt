Training successful robotic maniuplation policies within the space of PhysicalAI are bottlenecked by real-world data. 
Achieving acceptable completion scores for even the most straightforward tasks costs tens of thousands of hours of egocentric data, 
needed sufficient diversity for different objects, surfaces, and lighting. 

We describe a  pipeline that leverages Vision-Action-language models like NVIDIA Cosmos, along with OpenAI's mini-4o that produces 
variants of real egocentric clips while keeping the recorded actions, scaling a single video into hundreds of permutiations. 
We begin by using NVIDIA Cosmos 3 Nano to produce the Onotolgy of each clip, the task of each object's role, and plausable variations. 
We use Mini-4o to write our prompt, Segment Anything SAM2 generates image segmentaiton masks on the objects we wish to permute, while Cosmos 
Transfer 2.5 renders the items maintaing realism within the video. 

In this paper, we provide a primer that describes our process and findings, where structured reasoning about a scene produces robust realistic permutions. 
We also outline best practices and limitations for employing this process, demonstrating that this powerful and economical aproach is scalable when solving problems in robot learning.
