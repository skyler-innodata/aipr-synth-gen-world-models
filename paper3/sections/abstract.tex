\begin{abstract}
A robot policy trained on a few recordings of one kitchen learns that kitchen. Vision-language-action models get around this with very large demonstration sets, most of which exist to cover appearance. We describe a pipeline on NVIDIA Cosmos that turns a small set of egocentric household clips into appearance variants while keeping the recorded actions. Cosmos 3 Nano writes a structured ontology of each clip: the task, each object with its role and bounding box, and physically grounded variations for it. A language model filters the candidates and writes a prompt, SAM2 turns the boxes into tracked masks, and edge, depth, color and segmentation maps guide Cosmos Transfer 2.5. On 116 clips the reasoning stage yields 72 admissible variations per clip in about two minutes; a render takes 20 minutes on one A100, and we made 64. Single-surface recolors succeed, but the edit does not stay inside the mask: in our best render the protected region changes by a mean CIE76 $\Delta E$ of 35 against 75 for the target, and cluttered scenes recolor almost entirely. Action-level variation with Cosmos Predict2 produced no motion. Appearance variation is now cheap to propose and render; containing it, and generating new actions, are the open problems.

\keywords{Synthetic data \and World foundation models \and NVIDIA Cosmos \and Egocentric video \and Robot learning.}
\end{abstract}
