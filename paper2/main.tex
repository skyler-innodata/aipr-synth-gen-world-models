\title{Synthetic Video Generation for Robot Learning Using World Foundation Models}

\author{Shrey Jha \and Skyler Saucedo \and Frank Tanner}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A robot policy trained on a few hours of egocentric household video sees one counter, one bucket, one lighting condition, and cannot be expected to generalize beyond them. We describe a multi-model pipeline built on NVIDIA Cosmos that turns a small real video dataset into a large set of synthetic training clips by varying appearance while preserving the recorded actions. Cosmos Reason produces a structured ontology of each scene: the task, the objects and their roles, and physically grounded variations for each object. A language model turns the ontology into generation prompts and filters implausible variations, yielding about 70 candidates per 15-second clip. Edge, depth, color and segmentation maps, together with a subtraction mask that isolates the surface to be changed, guide Cosmos Transfer to render the variant. Appearance changes work for simpler scenes; complex, multi-texture scenes and action-level changes remain unsolved. From 116 clips the pipeline could produce about 8,120 variants at negligible language-model cost. Whether synthetic appearance data alone makes a robot more robust, or whether action generation must be solved first, is the question the work leaves open.

\keywords{Synthetic data \and World foundation models \and NVIDIA Cosmos \and Egocentric video \and Robot learning.}
\end{abstract}
